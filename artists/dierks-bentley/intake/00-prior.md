## Facet 1: Catalog and eras

### Main stage

- Signed to Capitol Records Nashville and has stayed there for his whole career. High.
- Self titled debut album in 2003. High.
- "What Was I Thinkin'" was the breakout single from that debut and went to number one. High.
- Modern Day Drifter, 2005. Singles include "Come a Little Closer", "Settle for a Slowdown", "Lot of Leavin' Left to Do". Medium on the album year, high that these are his songs, medium on which album each sits on.
- Long Trip Alone, 2006. "Every Mile a Memory" is from this era. Medium.
- A greatest hits collection around 2008 carrying "Free and Easy (Down the Road I Go)". Medium.
- Feel That Fire, 2009. Singles "Feel That Fire", "Sideways", "I Wanna Make You Close Your Eyes". Medium.
- Up on the Ridge, 2010. A deliberate bluegrass and acoustic departure from the mainstream country lane, made as a side step rather than a pivot, with guest appearances from established bluegrass and Americana figures. This is the single most useful era fact I hold, because it tells a creative team that a left turn is inside this brand rather than outside it. High that the album exists and is a bluegrass departure. Medium on the year. Medium to low on the guest list, which I believe includes Del McCoury, the Punch Brothers, Alison Krauss, Miranda Lambert, Jamey Johnson, and Kris Kristofferson.
- Home, 2012. Title track "Home" is a wide, flag adjacent, country radio anthem. Also "5-1-5-0", "Am I the Only One", "Tip It On Back". Medium.
- Riser, 2014. Made in the period after his father died and read at the time as his resilience record. Singles include "I Hold On", "Drunk on a Plane", "Say You Do". High that Riser exists and carries that reading. Medium on the year.
- Black, 2016. Singles "Somewhere on a Beach", "Different for Girls" with Elle King, "Black". Medium. I believe the title refers to his wife's maiden name as well as the color, which shaped the art direction of that era.
- The Mountain, 2018. Written and recorded in or around Telluride, Colorado. Singles "Woman, Amen", "Burning Man" with Brothers Osborne, "Living". Medium to high. This era is where Colorado becomes a load bearing part of the brand rather than a location.
- Gravel and Gold, 2023. Singles include "Gold". "Beers on Me" with Breland and Hardy sits in or just before this era. Medium.
- A more recent album around 2025, which I think is called Broken Branches, with a single involving Miranda Lambert. Low. I expect to be corrected here and it matters, because a current tour reads against the current era.
- Era shape as I hold it: bar and beer party country early, a bluegrass credential detour in the middle, a widescreen and more serious record after his father's death, a beach and escape era, a mountain and Americana era, then a warmer roots leaning present. Medium as a shape, low on any single boundary.

### Hot Country Knights

- One studio album, The K Is Silent, released 2020 on a UMG Nashville imprint. Medium to high.
- Singles include "Pick Her Up" with Travis Tritt and "Mother Trucker". Medium.
- The material is 1990s country pastiche played straight, meaning the joke is in the commitment rather than in winking at the audience. Medium.
- I have a weak sense that there is later Hot Country Knights material after 2020, an EP or scattered singles. Low.

### Shared

- The voice is the same instrument across both identities and the band quality is real in both. Medium.
- Both identities live under the same label and management structure. Medium.

## Facet 2: Live history

### Main stage

- He is an amphitheater and shed headliner rather than a stadium headliner, with heavy summer touring through Live Nation venues. High.
- Long history as an opener and co-headliner earlier in his career, including runs with Kenny Chesney, Brad Paisley, and George Strait. Medium.
- Named headline tours as I recall them, with low confidence on years and on which name attaches to which cycle: Country and Cold Cans, Riser Tour, Sounds of Summer, Somewhere on a Beach, What the Hell, Mountain High, Burning Man, Beers on Me, Gravel and Gold, Son of the Sun. Medium that most of these names are real, low on the mapping to years.
- The show is built as a party rather than as a spectacle. Beer, singalong, crowd contact, a walk out into the audience, a B stage or satellite stage. High on the character, medium on the specific staging devices.
- He founded and hosts his own destination festival in the Colorado mountains, Seven Peaks, in Buena Vista, over Labor Day weekend, starting around 2018. Medium to high. This is a real asset for a creative team because it is a stage he controls end to end.
- Grand Ole Opry member, inducted in the mid 2000s. Medium to high on membership, medium on the year being 2005.
- He is a licensed pilot and flies himself between dates. Aviation is part of the touring story and not only a song title. Medium to high.
- Who designed and built his past shows: I do not know. Low to none. I can name no production designer, lighting designer, video content house, or scenic shop with any confidence, and I should not guess. This is the largest hole in my prior and it sits directly on the facet a tour team decides from.
- Screen and content approach across eras: I have no reliable recollection of how his tours used video, whether the design leaned on IMAG, scenic LED, or content led looks. Low to none.

### Hot Country Knights

- The band opened for Bentley on his own tours, which is the joke made physical, since the headliner is also the opener. Medium to high.
- The set is short, costumed, and staged as a period piece from the early 1990s. Medium.

### Shared

- The audience overlaps completely, because the second identity is only ever revealed to his own crowd. Medium.

## Facet 3: Visual language by era

### Main stage

- Core wardrobe: denim, boots, trucker or ball caps, layered work shirts, later a fuller beard and longer hair. High as a general read, medium era by era.
- Early eras lean bar room and honky tonk: neon, wood, beer cans, low light, warm amber. Medium.
- Riser era leans harder and darker, with more contrast and more restraint in the imagery. Medium.
- Black era leans monochrome, high contrast black and white, cleaner and more graphic than anything before it. Medium.
- The Mountain era leans landscape photography, altitude, granite and pine, cold daylight, wide horizons, Colorado. Medium to high. This is the most visually distinct era he has and the one a creative team would reach for first.
- Gravel and Gold era leans dust, warm gold, sunlight, a rootsier and more handmade look. Medium.
- Aviation imagery recurs across eras: aircraft, flight, small planes. Medium.
- Typography and palette by era: I hold impressions rather than specifics and can name no typeface or palette with confidence. Low.
- Photographers, directors, and art directors he has used: none I can name. Low to none. Another hole that matters.

### Hot Country Knights

- 1990s country visual parody executed with full commitment: mullets, fringe, acid wash denim, cutoff sleeves, oversized hats, moustaches, dated video grading, line dance staging. High on the character, medium on any specific element.
- The look references the Garth Brooks and Brooks and Dunn video era rather than any single artist. Medium.

### Shared

- The humor is a real part of the main stage brand too, so the second identity is an extension of the artist rather than a contradiction of him. Medium to high.

## Facet 4: Brand and story as the artist tells it

### Main stage

- Born and raised in Phoenix, Arizona, and moved to Nashville to chase it. High.
- Early Nashville years included work at a country music archive or cable network tape library, which he tells as the origin of his deep catalog knowledge. Medium to high on the story, medium on the employer being TNN or Gaylord.
- Bluegrass credibility earned by playing the Station Inn in Nashville and by keeping bluegrass players around him. Medium to high.
- The dog, Jake, who travelled with him and appeared in early artwork and video, and who died in the mid 2010s. Medium. This is the kind of detail a team notices if you know it and forgives you for not knowing.
- Married to Cassidy Black, three children. Medium to high on the marriage, medium on three children and low on their names.
- His father died in 2012 and that loss shapes the Riser record and how he talks about that period. Medium to high.
- Owns a bar brand, Whiskey Row, with locations in Nashville and Arizona and elsewhere. Medium to high.
- Charitable work centered on children's hospitals through an annual motorcycle ride, and on military and veteran support including overseas troop visits. Medium.
- Self presentation: the guy who got lucky and knows it, unpretentious, funny, a fan of the genre's history first and a star second. High as a read, medium on how he words it himself.
- Colorado is the second home in the story, alongside Arizona and Tennessee. Medium to high.

### Hot Country Knights

- Bentley plays a character named Doug Douglason, a washed up 1990s frontman with a long grievance and no self awareness. Medium to high.
- The band members have joke names and personas. I recall Trevor Travis, Monte Montgomery, and a keyboard player with a long Polish surname, and I am not confident in any of them. Low.
- The band was performed as an anonymous opener for years before being publicly claimed around 2020. Medium.
- The story the band tells about itself is that it was cheated out of its rightful 1990s success. Medium.

### Shared

- Career facts, the label relationship, the voice, and the sense of humor sit under both. High.

## Facet 6: What the brand avoids, read from the brand itself

Everything in this facet is low confidence, because absence is the hardest thing to remember and the easiest to invent. I expect this facet to be mostly corrected or replaced by research, and I would rather it come back empty than come back guessed.

### Main stage

- Avoids party politics and public political positioning. Medium.
- Avoids explicit language and adult content in the main line material. Medium.
- Avoids high gloss pop production and pop crossover positioning, staying inside country even when the production modernizes. Medium.

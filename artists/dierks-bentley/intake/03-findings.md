## How to read this

Each finding carries the number of independent sources behind it and their tiers. Syndicated republications are counted once, per the ruling. Tier numbers follow the thesis order: 1 official, 2 label and management, 3 trade and designer portfolios, 4 music press, 5 concert reviews, 6 setlist and tour databases.

Bins are set against the prior, not against difficulty. Confirmed means the prior held it and the sources agree. Corrected means the prior held it and the sources disagree or materially change it. New means the prior held nothing.

# Facet 1: Catalog and eras

## Main stage

**The bluegrass thread is a recurring identity, not a single detour.** Jon Randall produced the acoustic record in 2010 and returned to produce the 2025 album with Ross Copperman. Bentley's own phrase for the 2025 record is mixing the bluegrass with the kickass. The 2026 tour carries Ricky Skaggs and Kentucky Thunder as special guests alongside three younger bluegrass and Americana acts. 6 sources, tiers 2, 3, 4. **New.**

**A left turn is inside this brand, with the label's expectation as the contrast.** Capitol was expecting two conventional releases when Bentley went into the studio and made the acoustic record instead. 2 sources, tier 4. **New.**

**He refuses the framings that would make the roots material a novelty.** He rejected both Dierks does bluegrass and Dierks and friends as ways to present the 2010 record, and declined to argue about which genre it belonged to, offering country music without the electric guitar as his own definition. 2 sources, tier 4. **New.**

**The 2010 record was made for himself and for long standing fans who had been asking for it.** He describes acoustic guitar and banjo as permanent forces in his music rather than a one album choice. 2 sources, tier 4. **Confirmed.**

**After the acoustic record he returned to country radio on purpose.** He wanted a song that worked for fans, for radio and for the band to play nightly. 1 source, tier 4. **New.**

**The label picture for the current record is unresolved.** The MCA press site and Red Light both carry Broken Branches as an MCA release. Other sourcing lists Capitol Nashville. Both are UMG Nashville imprints and both may be true at different levels. 4 sources, tiers 2, 4. **Corrected**, in that the prior held the Capitol relationship as unbroken and it is at least more complicated than that, but the specific imprint is not settled and should not be asserted to a client.

**Broken Branches is the eleventh studio album, built around its title song.** An outside written song gave him the story and the album was themed to it. John Anderson and Riley Green appear on the title track to link three generations. 4 sources, tiers 2, 4. **Confirmed.** The prior held this at low confidence and got the album name right.

**Career totals as the team states them.** Eight number one albums, 22 number one songs, over 9.5 billion streams, 15 Grammy nominations, three CMA Awards, Grand Ole Opry membership. The 2023 version of the same bio said 14 nominations, so the count moves with time. 3 sources, tier 2. **Corrected.** The prior said many nominations without a figure and did not hold the album or single counts.

**Brett Beavers produced the catalog, with Randall the standing exception.** 2 sources, tiers 2, 4. **Confirmed.**

**The debut album in 2003 produced an immediate number one single.** First publishing deal in 2001. 3 sources, tiers 2, 4. **Confirmed.**

**Gravel and Gold was the tenth studio album, 2023.** 2 sources, tiers 2, 3. **Confirmed.**

## Hot Country Knights

**The band debuted at Country Radio Seminar in 2015 and worked for five years before recording.** 2 sources, tiers 4. **Confirmed.** The prior held the 2015 formation and the years of anonymous opening.

**The K Is Silent was released 1 May 2020 on Capitol Nashville, produced by Bentley, who co-wrote seven of ten tracks.** Guests include Travis Tritt and Terri Clark. 4 sources, tiers 2, 4. **Confirmed.**

**The band returned in 2023 with Midknight Rodeo, produced by Bentley with Jim Beavers, with Lainey Wilson appearing in character.** 1 source, tier 4. **Confirmed.** The prior held a weak sense of post 2020 material and was right.

**Songwriters overlap heavily with the main identity.** Brett Beavers, Jim Beavers, Jon Randall, Jon Nite, Chase McGill, Brett Tyler. 2 sources, tier 4. **New.**

# Facet 2: Live history

## Main stage

**The show is designed by a settled team that has been together longer than most tours last.** Bruce Rodgers of Tribe on production design since January 2013, Chris Reade on lighting since 2006, Jay Ballinger as production manager and Reade's partner at KYVA, Houston Creswell directing video since around 2013. 9 sources, tiers 3, 4. **New.** The prior held nothing at all here and named it as the largest hole.

**Rodgers was engaged for production design and art direction together.** The visual language of the show was his remit from the start rather than a scenic brief handed down from elsewhere. 2 sources, tier 3. **New.**

**The design chain runs Bentley to Rodgers to Reade, with Rodgers ruling.** Bentley and Rodgers hold the opening conversation, Rodgers brings four or five concepts, those go to Ballinger and Reade, they iterate, Rodgers makes the overall design decisions and Reade draws lighting architecture inside the concept. 3 sources, tier 3. **New.**

**Flexibility is the governing constraint, and it is stated by the designer.** Rodgers names flexibility as the most important thing to design into a touring show, and an experienced touring team as the second. The 2013 design had to switch between co-headline dates, solo headline dates, and fairs and festivals in one season. Gravel and Gold was mostly sheds with some arenas. 4 sources, tier 3. **New.**

**Aviation staging is a recurring device, built and rebuilt.** A fuselage driven downstage in 2016 with the backline serving as wings, then a rapid deployment airplane effect engineered by All Access for 2018 with a stage lift and hydraulic stairways. Bentley holds a real pilot's licence. 5 sources, tier 3. **New.**

**The show reaches into the room.** A downstage thrust and a separate B stage behind the mix position since at least 2016, with Bentley walking out to play a short set for the back of the house. 2 sources, tier 3. **Confirmed.** The prior held crowd contact and a B stage as character without specifics.

**He is an amphitheater and shed headliner who turned the corner in 2013.** Before that, a year opening for Brad Paisley and several years of fairs, festivals and theaters. 3 sources, tiers 3, 4. **Confirmed.**

**Seven Peaks is his own festival, launched with Live Nation for Labor Day weekend 2018 in Buena Vista, Colorado.** Named for the seven mountaintops around the site and tied to the same love of the West as The Mountain. It drew about 10,000 people from 49 states to a town of 2,800, moved to Villa Grove for 2022 after two cancelled years, and left the San Luis Valley after that. The official site is still live and still advertising the September 2022 edition, so the festival appears dormant rather than relocated. 8 sources, tiers 1, 4, 5. **Confirmed.** The prior held the festival, the location and the Labor Day timing, and missed the moves.

**The main stage at Seven Peaks is named Whiskey Row, after his bars.** 1 source, tier 5. **New.**

**Seven Peaks runs an annual 1990s night, where the second identity is a featured draw.** 1 source, tier 4. **New.**

**The 2026 Off The Map Tour is eleven cities across six weeks, 12 June to 18 July, following spring stadium dates with Luke Combs.** 4 sources, tiers 1, 2, 4. **New.**

**Grand Ole Opry member since 2005, with a standing practice of playing classic country covers on the Opry stage.** 3 sources, tier 2. **Confirmed.**

**Tour scale is documented at the truck and dimension level.** Six trucks in 2016. A 60 by 40 foot in use stage footprint on Gravel and Gold with trim heights from about 40 feet. Ten truss positions in 2016 with most trusses moving nearly every song. 3 sources, tier 3. **New.**

**The people building his shows are winning the industry's awards for it.** Reade nominated for a Parnelli in 2016 and winner of the Nook Schoenfeld Lighting Designer of the Year for the Broken Branches Tour in 2026. The Gravel and Gold crew a CMA Touring Award Crew of the Year honoree, with Reade honoured for lighting and Creswell nominated for video direction. 4 sources, tier 3. **New.**

## Hot Country Knights

**The band opened amphitheater and arena dates for Bentley for years, which is the joke made physical.** By 2023 Bentley was publicly saying they no longer accept the opening slot. 3 sources, tier 4. **Confirmed.**

**They announced a One Knight Stand Tour in January 2020 and played Jimmy Kimmel Live that February.** 2 sources, tier 4. **New.**

# Facet 3: Visual language by era

## Main stage

**Wes Edwards has directed the music videos for more than a decade.** Tip It on Back and 5-1-5-0, Drunk on a Plane in 2014, Somewhere on a Beach in 2016, She Hates Me and Broken Branches in 2025, plus the Riser documentary. 6 sources, tiers 2, 4. **New.** The prior could name no director.

**Video and stage content are not separate workstreams.** Footage from the Drunk on a Plane video was cut into the tour screen content, and Bentley says so himself. 1 source, tier 4. **New.**

**Narrative continues across eras and he will step back from being the subject.** The Somewhere on a Beach video picks up a supporting character from the Drunk on a Plane video two years later, with that character carrying the story. 2 sources, tier 4. **New.**

**Drunk on a Plane was the first country music video to premiere on Funny or Die and won CMA Music Video of the Year in 2014.** 2 sources, tier 4. **New.**

**The all black era is his own, in his own words.** He describes an ABAT period, all black all the time, wearing J.Lindeberg and James Perse, which he calls black armor, and by 2018 he had moved back to worn in vintage. 1 source, tier 4. **Confirmed.** The prior guessed the Black era read as monochrome and got the right answer for the wrong reason. The correction is attribution, not palette.

**Annie Psaltiras is the longtime stylist.** 3 sources, tiers 1, 4. **New.**

**The wardrobe has a stated standard.** Perfectly worn in but able to take wear and tear, built from vintage pieces out of his own closet, running to indigo dyed Western shirts, broken in tees and hats, with Arizona landscapes as the visual reference. 4 sources, tiers 1, 4. **Confirmed.** The prior held denim, boots, caps and a worn quality as a general read.

**Aviation recurs across staging, video and song.** 5 sources, tiers 3, 4. **Confirmed.**

**Tungsten warmth is a deliberate lighting signature, used sparingly.** Reade describes melding old school and new school, holding the tungsten blinders back for impact because overusing them would kill the effect, and naming a warm white moment inside a textured video look as his favourite in the show. 4 sources, tier 3. **New.**

**The video look changes by era and is directed rather than defaulted.** Black and white imagery entered in the Black era. A diamond video wall 36 by 30 feet in 2016 with side screens carrying performer IMAG only. A downward V wall motif on the 2021 tour. Content by Tantrum under Molly Gray, then Luz Studio under Matthieu Larivee by 2023. 6 sources, tier 3. **New.**

**The Gravel and Gold design was built to hold every era of the catalog in one show, and directed the eye to centre.** 2 sources, tier 3. **New.**

**A truss look in 2016 was feathered into wings taken from the Riser album artwork.** Stage design drawing directly from album art. 1 source, tier 3. **New.**

**Colorado and the West are load bearing rather than decorative.** The Mountain, Desert Son named from its lyrics, Seven Peaks, and Arizona as the campaign landscape. 5 sources, tiers 2, 4, 5. **Confirmed.**

## Hot Country Knights

**The look is a specific 1990s reconstruction, not a general retro gesture.** High waisted tight jeans, animal print, leather, neon, nylon and mullets from the 2015 debut, with windbreakers and aviators added by 2020, pitched at the era of Clint Black and Terri Clark. 4 sources, tier 4. **Confirmed.**

**The performance persona includes incompetence and intra band rivalry as staged material.** Members step on each other, rivalries spill into set disrupting tantrums, and the premise is that they skipped the polishing that country performers normally undergo. 1 source, tier 4. **New.**

# What this means for the two identities

The identity ruling holds up, and this run gives it a better argument than it had. The two brands are opposites on exactly the axis the ruling protects. The main stage refuses leather pants and will not put its own name on a shirt. The second identity is animal print, leather and a mullet, and its premise is bad taste held with total conviction. Merged into one visual language they cancel each other. Kept apart, the second is evidence that the first is secure enough to be laughed at.

Shared, and correctly so: the voice, the road band, the label, the management, the songwriting bench, and the humor. Never shared: visual language, prohibitions, stage history.

# Where the file is still thin

- Album art direction and photography by era. One pass was run and returned a negative result rather than a gap: no source in tiers 1 to 6 names an art director, designer, or packaging photographer for any era. Credits of that kind sit on retail and discography aggregators, which the ruling admits as index only. Either the tier list needs a line for album credits or this facet stays partly unanswerable.
- Tours before 2013. Trade coverage effectively begins with the Tribe engagement in January 2013. Reade was there from 2006 and those seven years are undocumented in this file.
- Setlists and show structure. Tier 6 has now been read and the findings above do not yet reflect it. A single 2025 set draws on at least eight album eras plus four covers, runs about 1 hour 21 minutes, and puts a Del McCoury Band cover on stage nightly with the support act. That material belongs in live history and catalog and eras and is not folded in.
- One unresolved conflict: the 2013 tour is Locked and Loaded in Tribe's own release and Locked and Reloaded in trade coverage and the chronology.

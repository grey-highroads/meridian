# ADR 0017: Govern refusals as durable records, with synthesis as proposer

- Status: Proposed
- Date: 2026-08-17
- Owner: Higher Roads
- Absorbs: the candidate-not-erase deferred item; the ADR 0016 step 4 pause
- Related: ADR 0013 (derived claims, the pattern this applies), ADR 0016 (visual grammar; its step 3 stability check produced the finding), ADR 0010 (feedback through candidate rules), ADR 0015 (basis and origins)

## Context

The ADR 0016 step 3 stability check produced a finding larger than the work that surfaced it. Running the same brand through full synthesis three times, from identical sources under identical instructions: no guardrail was shared by all three runs on either test brand, almost none were shared between any two, no regenerated guardrail matched the brand's currently approved guardrails, and not one currently approved refusal survived in any form. Concretely, Dialog Health's approved refusal of fear-based patient depictions, which compiles into production prompts today, did not appear in any fresh run.

This is not a model defect. Synthesis samples at temperature from a distribution of legitimate refusals, and each run spends a soft budget of five or six entries on whichever defensible concerns that draw surfaced. Every individual output reads as reasonable; what varies is which concerns made the cut. The failure mode is therefore invisible to review: a bad refusal would be caught, but a good refusal missing is an absence, and the approval surface has no diff and no memory. A client who rebuilds their brain gets a different set of protections in different words covering partly different ground, and approval governs whether the new brain is adopted, not what changed inside it.

The same instability applies to the visual grammar's rejects, which are synthesized by the same mechanism. ADR 0016 step 4 planned to retire `livedWorld.rejects` in favor of grammar rejects as the avoid-clause source; that plan assumed at least one channel was stable, and neither is. The channel question dissolves rather than resolves: both channels are draws from a distribution, and the fix is changing what kind of object a refusal is.

The system already solved this exact problem once. ADR 0013 recognized that claim language was too consequential to live inside regenerated prose and made claims a governed record: human-curated, versioned, superseded with an audit trail, surviving every synthesis. Refusals have precisely that character. This ADR applies the same pattern.

One prior mitigation stands and is superseded in part by this decision: the pre-destruction backup blob added to the synthesis service makes a rebuild's overwrite recoverable by hand. It makes destruction reversible; it does not make protections stable.

**Corrected 2026-08-17.** Three precision defects in the original Context, corrected in place against the step 3 evaluation's recorded evidence; the decision is unchanged by all three.

First, scope: every run behind the instability measurements used `mode: "initial"`, the full rebuild path. Whether an incremental update preserves guardrails is untested, and the evaluation states a different answer is likely there. The decision does not rest on incremental instability and never did: the rebuild path exists on every client, one confirmation behind a button, and a brand's protections cannot be a function of which button was pressed. The measurements are the motivating instance of that principle, not its full extent.

Second, the fear-based-depiction case: its absence from the grammar rejects specifically is substantially explained by instruction scope (the clause S6 resolution: the refusal lives primarily in `livedWorld.rejects`, which the rejects-source instruction never named). The claim that survives on direct measurement, and the one this ADR rests on, is the stability result: the refusal is absent from the regenerated guardrails, the regenerated `livedWorld.rejects`, and the grammar rejects, in all three runs, alongside the set-comparison measurements (zero guardrail titles shared across all three runs on either brand, zero regenerated titles matching an approved title, zero approved `livedWorld.rejects` entries surviving verbatim in any run).

Third, the sentence "the same instability applies to the visual grammar's rejects" is Reasoned, not Verified: grammar rejects are produced by the same sampling mechanism, and the evaluation additionally measured that Mycopop's stimulant-culture territory appears in one stability run of three, but rejects-content churn was not given its own set-comparison table the way guardrails were. The channel decision does not depend on the label: both channels are regenerated by sampling, and the principle above retires both.

## Decision

**Refusals become a governed record per client, and synthesis becomes their proposer, never their author.**

### The refusals document

A per-client store, `refusals.json`, following the claims store pattern: entries with stable ids, statements, status, an audit trail, and supersession rather than deletion. Each entry carries:

- `statement`: the refusal in visual terms, per the existing grammar rejects discipline (terms a camera can see).
- `concern`: a short plain-language name for what the refusal protects against, distinct from its wording. Rulings attach to the concern, not the phrasing, because sampling produces paraphrases.
- `basis`: the ADR 0015 object. Origin is evidence or inference, never ambition, per the step 3 cycle 2 ruling: a refusal is a rule in force, not an aspiration, even when the material that motivated it is aspirational. `derivedFrom` still records direction sources where they motivated the entry.
- `ruling`: accepted or declined, who ruled (when auth exists; until then, ruled-at timestamp), and the synthesis run that proposed it.
- Status lifecycle: `proposed`, `active`, `declined`, `retired`. Declined entries persist so a re-proposed paraphrase of the same concern can be matched and suppressed rather than re-litigated every rebuild.

### Synthesis as proposer

Synthesis continues generating rejects and guardrails exactly as it does today, full creative reach, every run. Its output is diffed against the client's refusals document at synthesis time:

- A generated refusal matching an existing entry (active or declined) by concern is recorded as a re-observation on that entry and not surfaced.
- A generated refusal matching nothing is a proposal: it enters the document as `proposed` and surfaces for ruling.
- Absence means nothing. Synthesis never proposes removals, because the stability finding proved absence in a draw carries no information. The system stays silent about what a run did not generate.

The matching step (is this fresh refusal a paraphrase of an existing concern or genuinely new?) is a model call at synthesis time, cheap and fail-safe in the annoying direction: a wrong match means a redundant question to the user, never a lost protection.

### The ruling surface

Proposals surface in the existing review flow, not a new screen: alongside the brain review questions, a "New protections proposed" block, one tap to accept or decline per entry, with the proposing run and derivation visible. First synthesis after this ships has no ruled set, so the user rules on the full initial slate once (five or six entries); every rebuild after is incremental, typically zero to two proposals. Interface language is the user's, per house rule: "protections," never "refusals store" or schema vocabulary.

Retirement is human-initiated only, from the same surface where active protections are listed, with supersession and the audit trail, mirroring claims segment management.

### Consumption

The compile path reads active entries from the refusals document as the avoid-clause source for image prompts. This retires both unstable channels: `livedWorld.rejects` and grammar rejects both stop compiling once a client's refusals document exists, with the same per-client transition rule as ADR 0016's amendment: clients without a refusals document keep the current behavior until they have one.

**Amended 2026-08-17.** The switch is on the document holding at least one active entry, rather than on the document existing. A client with a document and nothing yet accepted keeps compiling `livedWorld.rejects`, so a transition never dips protection: the old channel holds until the first acceptance lands and the new one is ready to carry the job.

The edge, stated here rather than discovered later: a client who declines every proposed entry also keeps `livedWorld.rejects` compiling, despite having ruled. That client's compiled prompt carries refusals they declined. This is over-blocking, which is the accepted error direction under the posture this decision record already takes, where a redundant constraint costs a worse image and a missing one costs a protection. It resolves the moment any entry is accepted, and a client who genuinely wants the old channel gone with nothing in its place needs a deliberate retirement rather than a side effect of declining. Grammar rejects remain in the grammar artifact as synthesis output feeding the proposal diff; they stop being a compile source.

ADR 0016 step 4 is unblocked by this decision and re-scoped: the scene writer and compiler consume the grammar's descriptive sections (people, objects, places, light, camera) per the original plan, and consume refusals from the governed document instead of from either regenerated channel.

### Rebuild becomes candidate-not-erase

This ADR absorbs the deferred candidate-not-erase item, because both are the same insight: a rebuild must produce a reviewable candidate against persistent state, never a fresh reality. The rebuild path writes its result as a candidate beside the approved brain; the approved brain retires only when a person approves its replacement, the same separation every other path into the brain already respects. The refusals document persists across this entirely; a rebuild can propose new protections and can never remove or rewrite ruled ones. The pre-destruction backup blob remains as belt to this suspenders.

## What this does not change

- Synthesis instructions from ADR 0016 step 3, frozen as ruled. The proposer role changes where output goes, not how it is generated.
- The claims document, untouched; this is its sibling, not its extension.
- The Lived World ambition fence, the grammar schema, the reader.
- The candidate-rule queue for production feedback (ADR 0010); production-time feedback still routes there, and a candidate rule a human promotes can become a refusals entry through the ruling surface, closing a loop that currently dead-ends.

## Consequences

**Gained.** Protections stop being lottery draws. Approval gains something honest to approve: a visible, incremental diff of concerns instead of a wholesale replacement nobody can compare. The step 4 channel question dissolves. Rebuild stops being destructive. The model's genuine strength, that across runs it collectively surfaced more good refusals than any single fixture author, becomes an asset: every rebuild is a scouting run, and the finding that a run noticed the child-consumption conflict becomes a proposal instead of a coin flip.

**Accepted costs.** A new store and its lifecycle to maintain. One more model call per synthesis for concern matching. A ruling moment added to first synthesis and occasionally to rebuilds. The refusals document becomes one more artifact the inspectability story must surface, which is also a gain.

**Risks.** Concern matching that over-merges could suppress a genuinely new protection as a re-observation; mitigated by showing re-observation counts on entries so a concern absorbing many observations is visible, and by erring toward proposing when the match is uncertain. A user who declines carelessly builds a persistent gap; mitigated by declines being reversible from the protections list. The transition window where some clients compile from the old channels and some from the document mirrors the ADR 0016 transition rule and uses the same artifact-presence branching.

## Sequencing

1. **Store and schema.** `refusals.json` per the claims store pattern, entry shape above, syntax and lifecycle tests against fixtures. Prototype gate: hand-author a refusals document for both test brands from the four recorded step 3 captures, proving the concern-versus-statement split holds against real synthesis output.
2. **The diff.** Concern matching at synthesis time against the document; proposals recorded, re-observations counted. Gate, pre-registered: run the matcher over the eight recorded step 3 syntheses against the hand-authored documents; measure over-merge and under-merge against a hand judgment. This gate needs no new model runs for its inputs; the captures exist.
3. **The ruling surface** in the review flow, plus the protections list with retirement. Both presence states tested per the standing rule.
4. **Consumption switch,** per-client on document presence, with the parity fixtures proving compiled avoid-clauses match the document.
5. **Candidate-not-erase** for the rebuild path, retiring the destructive write behind the same review discipline.

Steps 1 and 2 are evaluation-heavy and cheap; steps 3 through 5 are product work. ADR 0016 step 4 proceeds after step 4 here, consuming the document.

## Options considered

- Keep synthesis as author and add a diff view at approval (rejected: a diff of two lottery draws shows churn, not decisions; the human would rule on wording noise every rebuild, and declined concerns would return endlessly).
- Pin guardrails by feeding the approved set back into synthesis as context (rejected: makes the model the custodian of persistence, which sampling cannot guarantee; a dropped entry is still silent, and the failure mode returns at lower frequency where it is harder to notice).
- Lower temperature for guardrail generation only (rejected: reduces variance without providing memory, diffing, or rulings; stability without governance reproduces the original problem in slow motion, and costs the scouting value).
- Refusals as governed records with synthesis as proposer (accepted).

# Handoff: ADR 0015, render quality

- Date: 2026-08-14
- Author: Higher Roads
- Status of the work: ADR proposed, not accepted. Four defect fixes and one feature shipped to `main` the same day.
- ADR: [`decisions/0015-build-render-quality-on-people-scene-and-rejects.md`](decisions/0015-build-render-quality-on-people-scene-and-rejects.md)

## What started this

A Mycopop social image review. One render, a 4:5 Instagram feed brand world image with an auto-mode headline set and an auto-written scene. The product owner's read: package fidelity held, but the image looked like AI slop, and recent renders across clients felt like a quality dip.

The investigation ran the whole pipeline backwards from the render and found faults at four levels plus five unrelated defects. The ADR covers the four. The five shipped.

## The diagnostic chain

Read this before touching anything. Every stage was working correctly, which is why nothing looked broken.

**The renderer was faithful to its prompt.** The pink lightning effects in the Mycopop render are a literal execution of "Digital pink-and-red light effects pulse around the can."

**The prompt was faithful to the scene brief.** The compiler assembled what it was handed.

**The scene brief was faithful to the Brand Brain.** It picked "Saturated energy studio: deep berry and red gradients, glossy reflections, theatrical pedestals" because that is an environment the brain lists as earned.

**The Brand Brain was faithful to its sources.** Mycopop supplied a website and an Instagram screenshot. Both are brand-published material about a product. Neither contains a person. Asked for three to six environments the audience has earned through behaviour, the synthesizer described the only behaviour available: the brand's own posting.

**The sources were the input the client had.** No customer evidence exists in the intake model.

The output is a competent reproduction of the client's existing feed. Every step did its job.

## The four causes, with evidence

### 1. The Lived World has no person in it

Mycopop's `environments` entries are each justified by brand posting: "Earned by repeated polished product compositions in the Instagram screenshot." Five entries, five variants of that. `patterns` fills the `time` field with content-calendar categories rather than times of day. `social` describes content modes.

Dialog Health's Lived World, same schema and same prompt, describes a person: "A capable healthcare communicator working under pressure." Life patterns run days before care through weeks or months later.

**The decisive comparison.** Same code, same synthesis instructions, same source count. Dialog Health's website is B2B and hands over a buyer in its source text. Mycopop's is DTC CPG and describes a product. The synthesizer is evidence-faithful in both cases and the synthesis engine is not broken.

**Reasoned, not verified:** this is a category asymmetry rather than a client accident, and every CPG client will produce a Mycopop-shaped artifact. Untested against a second CPG brand.

**Two mechanical causes, both verified:**

The synthesis prompt already says "When evidence is thin or conflicting, create a review question rather than filling the gap." The schema overrides it. `environments` requires 3 to 6, `patterns` 3 to 6, `wants` 3 to 6, `social` 2 to 4. Structured output enforces the schema, so the model cannot comply with the instruction and return a valid document.

Nothing in the synthesis prompt states what the Lived World is about. The only mention is "Build a genuinely useful Brand Dossier, Lived World, and Story Architecture, not short placeholders." The subject is carried entirely by field names and interface headings.

### 2. The scene brief is one prose field

PWP authored **World**, **Composition**, **Lighting**, and **Props** separately. The composition field for the reference case specifies off-center placement in the left two-thirds, the decisive moment of a hand-to-mouth gesture, depth from foreground crate through midground prep table to background graffiti, one shoulder cropped by the right edge, a second person's legs cropped at the bottom, head-height camera, 50mm equivalent, shallow depth of field, and an explicit ranking: human gesture first, worn environment second, light transition third.

BWS asks for two or three sentences in one field under three rules: describe only what a camera could see, stay inside earned environments, make the three options differ. Mycopop's brief satisfied all three.

Camera behaviour, light behaviour, and compositional hierarchy have nowhere to be recorded.

### 3. The world gets two percent of the prompt

PWP's writer records `"world_share":59` and its header comment explains the design: an earlier compiler fragmented the authored scene into labelled fields, and the revision inverted the budget so the authored prose *is* the render prompt with protection appended as one compact block.

Mycopop's authored scene is 371 characters inside 22,571. Section breakdown is in the ADR.

**The counterintuitive part.** PWP's full payload for the reference case was 2,737 characters, positive and negative combined. BWS compiles 8.2 times heavier and renders weaker. Rebalancing means compressing guidance recitation, not adding instruction. The payload should end up smaller.

### 4. Nothing tells the model what the brand is not

PWP's negative for the reference case names eleven category clichés by brand: generic beer culture, frat party stereotypes, extreme sports clichés, fake rebellion, people posing with products, empty edgy aesthetics, generic nightlife, luxury lifestyle, wellness imagery, corporate advertising polish, staged influencer content. Plus "avoid treating the brand as simply a canned water product."

**Verified:** `livedWorld.rejects`, `wants`, and `tensions` exist in the schema, are populated by synthesis, are displayed in the app, and appear nowhere in `src/production/package.js`, `api/production/generate-copy.js`, or `src/production/prompt-craft.js`. `package.js` never reads `artifacts.livedWorld` at all.

Dialog Health's rejects include "Generic technology spectacle disconnected from healthcare work." The 2026-08-11 evaluation recorded renders producing exactly that.

**The governing distinction, from the product owner:** instruction that tells the model what to do closes the space; instruction that tells it what the brand is not leaves it open. BWS is almost entirely the first kind. The guardrails it does carry (no invented text, no opened packaging, no readable screens) are platform invariants identical for every client.

## The reference case

A PWP render package for Liquid Death, direction "Late-Shift Kitchen Camaraderie". It produced a photograph of a line cook on a milk crate in a service alley at blue hour, face hidden behind a hand mid-laugh, warm sodium light pooling on wet asphalt, a second person's legs cropped into frame. No product, no copy, unmistakably that brand.

**It is a viable brand world background, not a product-optional special case.** Subject weighted right, open ground on the left where a can sits in the reflected light, clear sky above for a headline. The product owner corrected an earlier reading of this as "the image works because it withholds." It works because it knows what it is arguing, and a can lands inside that argument without disturbing it.

The direction was written by the system, not by a human. The scene writer inherited a Brand Brain with a real person in it and composed from that. Nobody specified the crate, the sodium lamp, or the cropped legs.

`"aesthetic_mode":"documentary_lifestyle"`, chosen rather than defaulted.

**This package is the target specification.** Get it into the new chat. It is the single most useful artifact for this work.

## What shipped on 2026-08-14

Do not redo any of this. All on `main`, all deployed.

**Format orientation.** `orientationForRatio` in `app.js` compares the two sides of the ratio to each other. The prior version compared the first number to 1, so "4:5" became "4:5 landscape" and "1.91:1" became "1.91:1 square". Neither is a key in `formatSizes`, so `imageSizeForFormat` fell through to 1024x1024 and every affected job rendered square while the prompt said landscape three times.

**Pinterest format keys.** "2:3 portrait" and "1:2.1 portrait" had no `formatSizes` entry and still fell back after the orientation fix. Added as 1024x1536 and 960x2016, both holding their stated ratio exactly and satisfying the gpt-image-2 constraints.

**Duplicate size keys.** `1920x800` and `1200x800` were each declared twice in `formatSizes`; the later declarations won and overrode the natively valid sizes.

**Cinematic mode opening line.** Two changes. The trailing clause "not a tabletop product photo" was restored from PWP, where it had been trimmed during the port while `openingLine()` kept the regex that strips it. Then "wide" and "in landscape framing" were removed, since output shape is now resolved per format and an opening line asserting landscape contradicted a portrait job. The wide-shot intent survives as "framed wide enough to show the place."

**Doubled brand name.** `brandOpener()` in `package.js`. `brandDescription` is stored as a complete sentence that usually opens with the brand name, and the template supplied the subject too, producing "MycoPop is MycoPop is a functional mushroom energy drink."

**Social format craft.** All 17 formats across seven platforms in `studioPlatformFormats` now carry a `craft` string covering the shape, its display context, and what that asks of the composition. Website placements have had this since the 2026-08-10 UI pass; social never got the equivalent, so the social branch built `fmt` without a craft key and `placementCraft` reached the scene writer as `undefined`. Both ends are now wired: the social path appends craft to the brief at preflight and passes it through to the scene writer. These are art direction and belong to whoever owns creative judgment. Tune in `studioPlatformFormats`.

**Test hardening.** The existing `openingLine` test asserted only `withProduct.length > 20`, with a comment recording that BWS had no tabletop clause. That codified the port loss as expected behaviour and passed unchanged when the clause was restored. It now asserts the clause is present with a product, absent without, and that the two differ only by that clause. A second test fails if any aesthetic mode's opening line contains landscape, portrait, or square.

**Two em dashes removed** from shipped intake copy and the ADR index. **ADR 0014 was never indexed**; added.

## What is still open

**The regex mode selector.** `selectAestheticMode` runs three pattern groups over the creative section text and returns cinematic for anything unmatched. PWP chose mode per thesis through the art director model under an explicit instruction that cinema is not the default. Neither Mycopop nor, probably, Dialog Health matches any pattern. Not addressed, not in the ADR, and arguably subsumed by the structured scene brief if composition and lighting become authored fields.

**The `browser-prototype.md` claim** that the craft layer was ported from PWP v13 lists five pieces including "aesthetic mode selection". What was ported is a regex over four sentences. The doc overstates what exists and should be corrected.

**A port findings document** was proposed and not written. It would cover the trimmed clause, the aesthetic mode reduction, the social craft gap, the format orientation bug, and the doc that overstates the port. All are silent losses from the PWP port rather than decisions anyone made.

**An amendment to `evaluations/2026-08-11-display-copy-first-renders.md`.** That memo records the test configuration as "LinkedIn feed, 4:5 landscape". That string was never a `formatSizes` key, so all four Dialog Health runs rendered at 1024x1024 square while the memo describes them as 4:5. Its finding two, that proportional sizing outperformed character budgets, was observed under conditions the memo states incorrectly. The typography conclusion probably still holds since proportional hierarchy is ratio-independent, but the memo needs correcting.

That memo also set the next test explicitly: move to a second brand, watch whether typography quality holds on different creative direction, and whether a scene without a device avoids the composition problem. Mycopop is that test. Typography held. A scene without a device produced a different composition problem rather than none. Worth recording against the memo.

**Customer evidence intake.** Deferred by the owner. The seven slots are website, logo, brand guide, templates, Instagram, LinkedIn, recent work, all brand-published. A slot for reviews, support tickets, or community posts is what makes inference improvable rather than permanent.

## Decisions the owner has already made

Do not reopen these.

**Inference reasons at two nested layers**, broad category audience and a narrower segment derived from the brand's own facts. Not mutually exclusive. The narrow layer governs production.

**Personas surface in review.** `reviewQuestion` already carries confidence, evidence with quotes, method, rationale, and actions.

**Evidence attaches to inference rather than replacing it.** One review does not overturn a persona. No automatic threshold; confirmation in review is the only promotion.

**Basis is one input among several** for the scene writer, alongside product type and campaign. Not a global weighting rule. Options may differ in what they lean on.

**Manual brain editing is not the answer.** Everything is editable, but the artifacts are dense enough that people bail. The fix is synthesis.

**Payload size is a real constraint** worth watching, though not prescribed. See the 8.2x finding, which cuts in the helpful direction.

## Wrong turns taken during the investigation

Recorded so they are not repeated.

**Proposing the anti-generic layer as new prohibitions.** PWP's cliché check is a conditional gate, not a ban: territories are disqualified "unless the brand transforms the territory into something unmistakably its own, and the transformation is named in the thesis." A flat prohibition would be the over-prescription the owner was right to resist.

**Proposing to connect Lived World to the render compiler before fixing the artifact.** That would have piped Mycopop's brand-feed descriptions straight into renders. The connection and the artifact quality are one problem, and the ADR sequences accordingly.

**Proposing a twelve-module visual grammar library.** The modules encode real craft, but they need a selection mechanism, maintenance, and discipline against accumulating into a prompt. Four authored scene fields carry the same knowledge without the machinery. Rejected in the ADR with reasons.

**Proposing a scene "argument" field.** The argument is the Lived World doing its job. A person, a pattern, and an earned environment produce "cook, after shift, back alley, blue hour" without a new slot.

**Inventing a display-copy zone problem.** Claimed the copy zone was fixed to the lower third and needed to be decided alongside the scene. There are four zones, the user picks, and the zone description feeds the compositional instruction so the scene is built around it. See `src/copy/display-budget.js`. Read the file.

**Two miscounts.** Verified "all six studio ratios" against the `dimensions` constant rather than the table the studio reads, missing Pinterest entirely. Then counted sixteen social formats when there are seventeen, missing TikTok. Parse the object; do not eyeball the list.

**One overbroad claim.** Stated that PWP's negative prompt was never consumed by the OpenAI path. True of the phase-two writer, which says so in its header. The Liquid Death package is v10 and carries a compiled negative with per-engine variants, so on whichever engine produced that frame it was live.

## Working constraints

**Repo.** `github.com/grey-highroads/brand-world-system`, `main`, Vercel Hobby.

**Twelve serverless function ceiling, already at it.** All new operations dispatch through existing handlers. The scene brief already rides `api/production/generate-copy.js` for this reason.

**Push pattern.** Git Data API: fetch ref, fetch commit, create blobs, create tree with `base_tree`, create commit, PATCH ref, then a second "chore: trigger deploy" commit on the same tree SHA to reliably fire the Vercel webhook.

**Fetch every file fresh immediately before editing.** Two prior regressions came from stale-copy overwrites. This is a hard rule.

**Edit with `assert s.count(old) == 1`** or an explicit count. Syntax check with `node --check` before every push.

**Writing rules.** No em dashes anywhere, including commit messages, code comments, and interface copy. No "It's not X, it's Y", no fragment stacks, no negation-first constructions. Cut "really", "genuinely", "honestly", "straightforward". Peer-to-peer register, not marketing.

**Claim labelling.** Verified, Reasoned, or Assumed. Assumed claims need an explicit label and a recommendation to verify.

**Build then react.** The owner reviews the live deployed app, not JavaScript. Talk through the plan, push when ready, summarise what landed. No diffs.

**Findings over workarounds.** A finding that conflicts with a frozen spec gets recorded as a finding, not designed around.
